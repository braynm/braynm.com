How I Ended Up Building My Own Investing App

# From MP2 and Philippine REITs to Interactive Brokers, and the app I built to make sense of it all.

I started taking personal finance seriously in my 30s. I was starting a family and had a child to think about. I wanted to understand how to save and prepare for our future, but I didn’t know much about finance.

Like many people, I started with YouTube and Facebook. Videos about saving and investing introduced me to Pag-IBIG MP2, which became one of my first steps beyond a regular savings account. From there, I became interested in Philippine REITs like RCR and AREIT. I began learning about dividends, share prices, and company disclosures.

Each step gave me something else to look into. Eventually, I wondered whether I could invest in US companies from the Philippines. After researching brokers, funding, and fees, I found Interactive Brokers.

Opening IBKR for the first time was overwhelming. There were panels, charts, order types, and settings I didn’t understand. I could access the market, but even finding my way around the platform took work.

As I continued learning, I wanted a place where I could bring the information together in a way that made sense to me. I knew how to build software, so I started working on my own app.

It’s called IBKR Screener. It connects to Interactive Brokers and brings screening, charts, research, and trade management into one workspace.

The main features follow the things I want to understand before making a decision:

  • Filings: Read SEC disclosures to see what a company has reported, from financial results to offerings and ownership changes.
  • Indices: Explore companies in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100, compare their performance, and see how different sectors are doing.
  • Fundamentals: Look at revenue, profitability, and valuation to understand the business behind the stock price.
  • Charts: Explore price and volume across timeframes, mark important levels, and compare views side by side.
  • Order routing: Send orders to Interactive Brokers from the app and track their status alongside my positions.

News adds context, and the journal gives me a place to review my decisions afterward.

That’s the part I wanted to connect: finding something interesting, understanding it, deciding what to do, and being able to look back afterward.

The app is built with Elixir, Phoenix LiveView, and PostgreSQL. LiveView keeps the screens updated as market data arrives, and the IBKR integration connects it to my broker.

I’ve also been exploring Claude Code while building it. AI assistance is a big part of how I’ve been able to cover this much ground. Building all of these features on my own would have taken much longer. This project has been a way to explore what I can build with that help, while still taking responsibility for understanding and checking the result.

Building it for myself means I can adjust things as I go. If I keep looking for the same information, I can make it easier to find. If something takes too many steps, I can simplify it.

I started this journey wanting to understand money better for my family. Somewhere along the way, it became a software project too. I’m still learning about the market, and now I have something I can keep improving as I learn.
